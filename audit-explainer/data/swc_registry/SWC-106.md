# SWC-106: Unprotected SELFDESTRUCT Instruction

## Summary
A function that calls `selfdestruct()` (destroying the contract and
forwarding its remaining ETH balance to an address) is reachable by anyone,
with no access control restricting who can trigger it.

## Why it matters
Anyone can permanently disable the contract and redirect its funds,
effectively bricking the protocol and stealing its balance in one
transaction. This has been used in real incidents to freeze funds in
contracts that depended on a library contract that could be self-destructed.

## Typical patterns Slither flags
- `selfdestruct(...)` or the older `suicide(...)` alias reachable from a
  public/external function without an owner or role check.

## Typical fix
- Restrict the function to a trusted role via a modifier.
- Consider whether `selfdestruct` is needed at all - many modern designs
  avoid it entirely given its sharp edges (including EIP-6780 changing its
  semantics in recent network upgrades).
